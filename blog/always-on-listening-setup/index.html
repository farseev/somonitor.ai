How to set up always-on social listening that people use

6 Jul 2026 · 7 min read · Somonitor editorial team · FAQ

Always-on social listening is a continuous programme that monitors conversation about your brand, competitors and category, then turns it into regular decisions. It works when it has sharp queries, named owners, a fixed rhythm of reviews and alerts tuned to matter. Without those, even the best dashboard becomes a tab nobody opens.

Why do listening programmes fade?

Many teams have been here. A listening tool is bought with enthusiasm, a dashboard is built, a launch meeting is held. Three months later, nobody opens it. When a crisis arrives, someone scrambles to search manually.

The cause is rarely the technology. It is a programme designed as a reporting project rather than a habit. Somonitor's view: always-on listening is an operating rhythm first and a tool second.

What does a working programme need?

Definitions

  • Query: the set of terms, names, misspellings and exclusions that defines what you listen to.
  • Listening scope: brand, competitors, category topics and communities you deliberately monitor.
  • Rhythm: the fixed schedule of reviews: daily scan, weekly brief, monthly deep dive.
  • Owner: the named person accountable for reading, interpreting and routing each signal.
  • Alert: an automated notice triggered by meaningful change, not routine volume.

How do you set it up, step by step?

  1. Write down the decisions listening should inform. Positioning, product fixes, crisis response, content planning. If you cannot name the decision, you will not use the data.
  2. Define scope. Your brand and products; three to six competitors; two or three category topics; any communities you know matter.
  3. Build honest queries. Include misspellings, nicknames and local slang. Exclude obvious false positives, such as other meanings of your brand name. Review results and refine weekly for the first month.
  4. Agree the rhythm. A ten-minute daily scan of alerts and new themes; a weekly one-page brief; a monthly review of share of conversation and tensions.
  5. Name owners. One person owns the programme; each signal type routes to someone who can act on it.
  6. Tune alerts. Start with sensitive topics only. See our guide to crisis early warning for the signals that matter.
  7. Close the loop. Every monthly review lists actions taken because of listening. If the list is empty, change something.

What should the weekly brief contain?

Keep it to one page. Somonitor's weekly brief, written using SOMIN's concept engine, follows this structure, and it works just as well by hand:

  • What changed: two or three shifts in themes, emotions or share of conversation.
  • Why it matters: one line each on the implication.
  • Evidence: links to the posts behind each point.
  • Suggested action and owner.
  • Watch list: early signals not yet worth action.

The evidence links are not decoration. They are what lets busy colleagues trust the brief in seconds. We explain why in making AI conclusions checkable.

A worked example

A regional retailer sets up listening with the decisions written down first: which store issues to fix, which product lines to promote, and how to respond to complaints. Queries include store nicknames used locally. The weekly brief goes to operations, merchandising and comms. In the first month, the brief surfaces repeated complaints about one store's queue times and enthusiastic posts about a little-promoted product range. Both are routed to owners. That is the moment listening becomes a habit, because people saw it lead to action.

Who should be involved?

Listening that sits only with the social team stays a social metric. Involve insight, product, customer service and leadership in the monthly review. Agencies can run the rhythm on a brand's behalf; our sister agency AgentC builds agent-run workflows of this kind, with human strategists owning judgment. What are the common mistakes?

Three come up again and again. First, queries that are too broad, which drown the team in irrelevant posts and teach them to ignore the tool. Second, too many alerts too early, before anyone knows what normal looks like. Third, briefs written for the social team alone, so insight never reaches the people who can fix products or policies. Each is easy to avoid if you start narrow, tune slowly and share widely.

A listening programme is not measured by its dashboard. It is measured by the decisions it changed.

Setup checklist

  • Decisions listening will inform are written down.
  • Scope covers brand, competitors, category and communities.
  • Queries include slang and misspellings, and are refined weekly at first.
  • Daily, weekly and monthly rhythms are scheduled with owners.
  • Alerts start with sensitive topics only.
  • Monthly reviews record actions taken.

Get the rhythm right and the tool becomes indispensable. That is what Somonitor is designed to support.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to set up always-on listening?

Initial scope and queries can be ready within days. Expect a month of query refinement and rhythm-building before the programme feels reliable. The habit matters more than the speed of setup.

Who should own social listening?

One named programme owner, often in insight, comms or social, with clear routing of signals to product, service and leadership. Shared ownership without a single owner tends to mean no ownership.
